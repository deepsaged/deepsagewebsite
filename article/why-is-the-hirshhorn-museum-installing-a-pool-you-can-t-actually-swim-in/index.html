Why is the Hirshhorn Museum installing a pool you can't actually swim in?

A new, permanent installation in D.C. is bringing a dry, sculptural swimming pool to the sculpture garden, and no, you can't bring your floaties.

deepsage.com/s/BNElqR

Key Takeaways
  • A permanent, waterless pool is coming to the Hirshhorn Museum.
  • The work uses luxury aesthetics to explore themes of space and status.
  • It officially debuts on October 31 in D.C.

Have you ever looked at a luxury swimming pool and thought, 'This would be much more profound if it were physically impossible to use'?

If not, you clearly haven't spent enough time staring at contemporary art.

Starting October 31, the Hirshhorn Museum in Washington, D.C. is unveiling 'Eternally Unswimmable' by the duo Elmgreen & Dragset. It's a permanent installation in the museum's sculpture garden that mimics the look of a high-end pool, but with one tiny, inconvenient catch: there is no water. It is, as the title suggests, eternally unswimmable.

The vibe is 'Luxury, but make it existential'

If you've ever scrolled through Instagram and felt a pang of jealousy at a billionaire's poolside brunch, this installation is for you. Elmgreen & Dragset are masters of taking the glossy, hyper-sanitized aesthetics of modern wealth and injecting them with a heavy dose of 'wait, what is the meaning of life?'

By placing a dry, architectural pool in a public garden, they aren't just playing a prank on tourists looking for a place to cool off. They are engaging in a bit of conceptual art commentary regarding how we consume space and status. The pool looks inviting, it looks expensive, and it looks like the perfect place for a summer cocktail-until you realize it's just a concrete slab designed to make you contemplate the void. It's basically the architectural equivalent of a 'Do Not Touch' sign on a museum painting, but much more stylish.

Why this isn't just a dry prank

I know what you're thinking: 'Great, another piece of art that's just a fancy way of saying "nothing is real."' And honestly? You're not entirely wrong. But there is a deeper layer to the sculpture garden installation that goes beyond mere trolling.

Contemporary art often uses familiar architectural elements to disrupt our expectations of public spaces. When we see a pool, our brains automatically trigger a sequence of 'splash, swim, relax.' When that sequence is interrupted by a dry, empty basin, it forces a momentary glitch in our perception. It turns a site of leisure into a site of observation.

Unveiling Date

The installation will be officially revealed to the public on October 31.

The 'So What?' for your weekend plans

So, what does this mean for you, the person who just wanted to walk through a nice garden and not have a mid-life crisis?

  1. No splashing allowed: You can't bring your kids to jump into this one. It's a zero-splash zone.
  2. Great for photos, bad for lounging: If you're looking for a backdrop for your next 'living my best life' selfie, this is perfect. Just don't try to lean too hard on the edge if you're feeling particularly dramatic.
  3. A new way to view D.C.: The Hirshhorn is already a heavy hitter in the art world, but adding a permanent, provocative piece like this ensures the sculpture garden remains a place of active conversation, even if those conversations are just people asking, 'Wait, is there really no water?'

At the end of the day, 'Eternally Unswimmable' is a reminder that art doesn't always need to provide a service or a way to cool down. Sometimes, its only job is to stand there, looking beautiful and slightly disappointing, and ask you why you expected anything else.

Next time you see something in life that looks perfect but is completely inaccessible, will you be annoyed, or will you appreciate the aesthetic?

Glossary
Conceptual Art

Installation Art
Large-scale, site-specific artworks that transform a space and often invite viewer interaction.
